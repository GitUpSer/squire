Questions

The short version.

What is Squire?

Squire is an AI personal assistant you talk to over text messages. It remembers your people, dates and preferences, sets reminders, triages your email, manages your calendar, researches things online, and follows up on standing tasks — and it asks before doing anything consequential. There is no app to install; it is a new contact in Telegram (WhatsApp soon after).

How is Squire different from ChatGPT or Claude?

ChatGPT or Claude is the brain — Squire runs on the AI plan you already pay for. Squire adds what a chat window lacks: long-term memory across conversations, scheduled and recurring tasks, integrations with your email, calendar and tools, and a private environment of its own per user, all reachable from your messaging app.

Which messaging apps does Squire work on?

Telegram at launch, with WhatsApp following shortly after. You never install anything — your assistant messages you first.

What can Squire connect to?

Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Outlook, GitHub, Notion, Slack, Todoist, Spotify, Uber, OpenTable and Amazon, with more being added. Connecting anything is optional and done on your say-so.

Is Squire private?

Each Squire runs in its own sealed environment with its own keys, and your conversations live only there. The service is built with nowhere central to store your messages — no master inbox and no admin view — and automated checks on every change fail the moment anything could leak.

How much does Squire cost?

There is a free trial at launch. Pricing will be announced when Squire launches; waitlist members hear first.
